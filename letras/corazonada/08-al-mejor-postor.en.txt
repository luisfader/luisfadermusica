How much is your flag worth?
How much is your brother worth?

Your engines roar, you kick up the dust,
you think you own the desert and whatever's left.
But the one who pays you puts you on a leash,
and you bark happily every time he walks you.
You sold the girl you found in the mine,
you traded her for gasoline, for bullets and flour.
You said "it's business, that's life",
and someone else's life became your merchandise.

To the highest bidder, to the highest bidder,
you sell them your rifle, you sell them your courage.
To the highest bidder — how much is your voice worth?
The one who buys you today will sell you out tomorrow.

Today you serve the one below, the one who lives without a sky,
tomorrow the one on tank treads coming over the ground;
you change your flag like you change your T-shirt,
any boss will do if he fills up your cartridge belt.
They told you "you're free" and put a price on you,
they told you "you're strong" and used you like scrap.
When the missiles come, who's going to look after you?
The boss has a bunker; you've got nothing but sand.

To the highest bidder, to the highest bidder,
you sell them your rifle, you sell them your courage.
To the highest bidder — how much is your voice worth?
The one who buys you today will sell you out tomorrow.

But I know there are kids who were born in the ruins,
nobody gave them a book, only a carbine.
If one day you get tired of having a price on you,
there's a turquoise wall that charges nothing to get in.
Nobody's sold there and respect can't be bought:
bread is shared in the square and they learn the alphabet.
Break the leash, shut off that engine:
nobody belongs to anybody… not even to the highest bidder.

To the highest bidder, to the highest bidder,
don't sell them your rifle, don't sell them your courage.
Nobody has a price, nobody belongs to a boss:
the one who buys you today will sell you out tomorrow.

How much is your brother worth?
Nothing.
He has no price.
