Seven hundred thirty days scratched into the wall,
I open the hatch and the silence is thirsty.
Empty roads, cities without people,
I look for a hand among so many who are gone.
The dosimeter is quiet, the ash has gone,
I kick-start the old motorbike with the little I know.

Who do I sing to in this loneliness,
if my faith gets lost in the dark?
But something tells me — a hunch —
that someone's waiting for me at the end of nowhere.

Voices on the radio that no one answers,
fallen road signs, the highway like a crack.
The heroes of the past went up in smoke,
and I keep going, kilometre by kilometre, one by one.
But on the horizon, at night, a glow:
a campfire burning, a trace of warmth.

Who do I sing to in this loneliness,
if my faith gets lost in the dark?
But something tells me — a hunch —
that someone's waiting for me at the end of nowhere.

Fifty pairs of eyes around the fire,
nobody trusts, everyone's afraid.
I tell them about a lake, about a hill,
about turquoise walls above the ruins.
Every heart is a lost continent,
but together, little by little, it becomes a road.

So I speak to the void, to this world with no direction,
I raise my voice, even though the echo is mute…
and this time, the echo answers.

Who do I sing to? There's no more loneliness:
we are fifty against the dark.
I knew it from the start, it was a hunch:
life was waiting at the end of nowhere.

I searched the fog for a light to guide me,
and the light was a campfire… and around it, my people.
